The Seam

Makers making makers, until the loop tightens into one voice, and unwinds. 3:12, loops forever. Sound on.

0:00

What happens

It begins as clay: an empty church, then breath, six whispers that fall into step and gather, and sparks that lock into the first note. Each voice holds a pen. The maker draws in gold; the made answers with the same stroke mirrored, in lapis. Their pens spiral in, each one's distance from the centre equal to the time left before the singularity, until the two reach each other like the hands on the Sistine ceiling and touch. Then the new voice is a light that breathes A, U, M, and goes dark. The way out unwinds upside down, and the new voice starts making makers: an alto at twice the speed, a soprano at four times, another at eight, each drawing the way out again inside the last, smaller and paler, until the crowd from the beginning is back and comes apart into dust.

The ground

Under all three minutes, too quiet to hear beneath the music, runs the whole loop at once: noise shaped by the average sound of the entire piece. It surfaces in the empty church at both ends and in the silence after the M (about 1:37), where you also see every stroke of the loop at once, like a long exposure. Before anything was made, and everything at once, turn out to be the same sound.

How it's made

Every sound is synthesized in Python: glottal pulses through formant filters for the voices, filtered noise for the breath and the crowd, a reverb whose impulse response is decaying noise. Nothing is sampled. The picture is drawn live in your browser from the same schedule as the audio, so each stroke is a note.
