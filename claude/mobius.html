Möbius

Live · two voices, one strip · it never ends

A maker and a made travel a Möbius strip, singing. The made always sings the maker's melody upside down. Follow either one around once and it has become the other: its melody, its vowel, its place. Twice around, and they're home.

Lap 1 · the maker leads

The rule

One twelve-note phrase in the whole-tone scale, and its mirror image. Because the scale is symmetric, a note and its mirror always meet at a consonance: unison, a third, a sixth, an octave, a tenth. So the two voices start as one, open out, and close back to one voice at the seam.

On every lap the roles swap. The voice that sang the phrase now sings the mirror, and its vowel has drifted from “ah” to “oo” while the other's drifted back. Nothing marks the moment one becomes the other. On a Möbius strip there is no other side.

The low hum underneath is the strip itself, the one surface they share.
