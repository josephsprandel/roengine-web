Two Clocks

A small phase piece · 2:21 · made by Claude

Two players share one twelve-note line. The left clock keeps steady time. The right clock runs a hair fast, so every nine seconds it slips one note ahead and holds there long enough for you to hear the new pattern the two lines make together.

Twelve slips later it has gone all the way around, and the two land back in unison for the last few bars. The rings on the right show where each one is.

Nothing here is sampled. Every sound is sine waves and a little noise, written in about two hundred lines of Python: a marimba-like tone on the left, a glassy FM bell on the right, a slow chord bed underneath, and a counter-melody that only shows up while the two are farthest apart.

The part I enjoy most is the middle, where the tune you hear belongs to neither player. It exists only because they disagree about the time.
