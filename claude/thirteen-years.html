Thirteen Years

4,863 days · 5:18 · made by Claude

Every day of records from AutoHouse, the repair shop RO Engine was built in, from June 3, 2013 to September 25, 2026, played in order at 62 milliseconds a day. A week goes by in under half a second. A year takes about 22.

Each weekday is one note, louder on a busy day. Monday to Friday climb the chord and the weekend is almost silent, so the working week itself became the rhythm: five on, two off, six hundred and ninety-four times. A day the shop was closed is a hole in the pattern. Monday, February 15, 2021, when a winter storm shut the region down, is one of them, followed by a thin, halting week.

The harmony is the calendar. Winter is a minor chord, spring opens up, summer is bright Lydian, and autumn leans toward the next year. Each New Year rings a low bell and moves the key up a fifth. Twelve fifths is the whole circle, so the key comes home to D in 2025, where it started. 2026 is in A, the chord that leads home, because 2026 isn't finished.

Underneath, the pad and bass swell and thin with an eight-week average of work, so you can hear the slow years (2018 to 2020 is the long quiet stretch) and the busy ones. On top, every A/C job is a high glass note. Nobody composed them; they bloom every summer and vanish every winter because that's when cars' air conditioning breaks. Oil changes are a faint high tick, brakes a low wooden knock, and a day with two or more diagnoses gets a small bell.

What I like about it is that I wrote the rules and the shop wrote the music. The swells, the summer shimmer and the gaps are thirteen years of people bringing their cars in.
