2. What the integration does

When someone at your shop connects QuickBooks Online in Settings → Accounting, RO Engine uses Intuit's standard sign-in (OAuth 2.0) with the QuickBooks accounting permission only. Depending on the mode you choose, it then:

  • Reads your chart of accounts, tax codes, payment methods, customers, items and company accounting preferences, so you can choose which accounts our entries post to, and reads back the invoices and payments it wrote to confirm they were recorded as sent.
  • Writes, for repair orders closed on or after the start date you pick: an invoice per repair order with its payments (Detailed mode), or one summary journal entry per day (Daily summary mode). It creates the customers, the "RO Engine" items and, if you ask, the accounts those entries need. When a repair order is reopened, voided or a payment is reversed in RO Engine, it updates, voids or removes the matching record, or posts a dated correction rather than editing a posted day.
  • Timing. RO Engine checks for new entries about once a minute. In Daily summary mode a day's entry is posted after that day ends. A correction is posted as its own entry, dated the day of the change; a posted day is never edited.
  • Never reads or changes bank feeds, bank reconciliation, payroll, or records it didn't create, other than the reads above. It never moves money.

By default that is the shop's owners, and your shop decides who else gets it and is responsible for that choice. Intuit's own sign-in then requires the person to sign in to the QuickBooks Online company and approve the connection there. By connecting, that person confirms they are authorized to act for the shop and to grant RO Engine access to that company.

  • The access tokens Intuit issues to us are stored encrypted and used only by your shop's RO Engine, only for the reads and writes described in section 2.
  • Besides the tokens, we keep only what the integration needs to keep your books consistent: your account choices, and the QuickBooks record numbers that match each RO Engine invoice, payment, customer and item (so a later change updates the right record instead of creating a duplicate). These are kept with your shop's RO Engine data, and RO Engine's assistant features can see the record numbers as part of your shop's data.

  • Records the integration wrote to QuickBooks stay in your QuickBooks company after you disconnect or leave RO Engine; managing them is up to you.

5. Your responsibilities

  • If QuickBooks rejects an entry (for example, a closed accounting period), RO Engine marks it for attention in Settings → Accounting rather than forcing it; someone at your shop needs to resolve it.
  • Keep your QuickBooks and RO Engine sign-ins secure.

6. Disconnecting

Your shop can disconnect at any time in Settings → Accounting, or revoke access from your Intuit account's list of connected apps. When you disconnect in RO Engine, it immediately asks Intuit to revoke our access, deletes the stored access tokens, and stops sending anything to QuickBooks.

  • RO Engine keeps recording your accounting activity in your shop's account, and holds it unsent. If you reconnect to the same QuickBooks company, it picks up where it left off; otherwise nothing more is sent.
  • Your account choices and the matching record numbers stay with your shop's RO Engine data, and are deleted along with it when that data is deleted under your agreement with us for RO Engine.
  • Records already written to QuickBooks stay there (section 4).

These terms end for your shop when you disconnect, or when your RO Engine subscription ends. Sections 4, 5, 8, 9, 11 and 13 continue to apply after that.
